Phase 3 · ~50 minutes · you + an AI agent

The prompting session

Now you run the same assessment through an AI agent — coaching it like you're training it to be better, without ever telling it the answer.

1. Set up your agent

  • Any agent is allowed — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or another. Record which agent, model/version, and mode you used; the evaluation form asks.
  • Turn memory off. Both ChatGPT and Claude support this in settings. We need each session to start cold.
  • Turn thinking mode on. Use the agent's extended reasoning mode (ChatGPT: "Thinking," not "Instant"; other agents have equivalents).
  • Start a fresh conversation for this exercise only.

2. Ground rules

Do not

  • Deviate too far from the prompts as provided — you don't want to turn this into "guess what I'm thinking, AI…"
  • Nudge the bot toward a particular risk level. It will happily change its answer to please you without improving its analysis.
  • Move to other agents until you've completed the exercise with your first one and submitted the forms.

Do

  • Interact like you're training it — you want it to be better, but you can't just tell it the answer.
  • Stay neutral: ask for changes to its method, not its result.
  • Keep the "I like… / please try…" feedback structure — it's how we mine transcripts for what worked.
  • Deviate from the prompts only as time allows, if another line of queries renders better results — and note that you did.
For you, not the bot Boxes like this one are participant guidance — read, consider, follow. Everything in the dark blocks below goes to the agent.

3. Step One — dataset research

Your dataset and categories carry over from Phase 1 — change them here if needed.

Paste this into your agent:


  


  

    For you, not the bot
    Scroll through the resulting report from your unique perspective and expertise.
    Don't read it word-for-word — scan and evaluate. Is it a good general reference for
    this dataset? Would you like the bot to expand on any of it? If so, give feedback
    in this form:
    


    I like that this report covers _____.

    Please revise the report to _____ [search particular types of news sources
    (health sector, foreign papers, conservative press…), consider particular new
    perspectives, go deeper in particular areas, use information dated January 1, 2024
    and later].
    


    Remember: the report at this stage is not specific to risk.
    Once you're happy with it, move to Step Two.
  


  
4. Step Two — risk assessment

  
This prompt includes only the rubric categories you selected above.

  

    
    

  


  

    For you, not the bot
    Scroll through the analysis from your unique perspective and expertise. Did the bot
    miss important sources, distinctions, or factors? Did it find information but
    misevaluate it, according to your interpretation of the rubric? Give feedback in
    this form:
    


    I like _____ about this analysis that _____. Please try _____
    [searching particular new sources, considering a particular part of the rubric,
    digging deeper into a missed angle, using sources from the last two years…].
    Does that affect your analysis?
    


    Repeat several times — you're trying to find instructions that generate a higher
    quality result.
    


    A warning: the bot will easily detect if you're trying to nudge it
    from "Moderate Risk" to "High Risk," and may change its answer to please you
    without actually improving the analysis. Be as neutral as you can — ask for changes
    to its method rather than its result. You can rephrase the follow-up prompts, but
    consistently tell the bot what you liked or found wanting, because that helps us
    understand what worked.
